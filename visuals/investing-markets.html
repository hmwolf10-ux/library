Every investment is a trade-off: higher expected return = higher risk. The scatter plot below shows the historical risk-return profile of major asset classes. The efficient ones cluster along the Capital Market Line. Anything below it is a bad deal for the risk taken.

3. Modern Portfolio Theory — Efficient Frontier

Harry Markowitz's insight (1952): you can reduce portfolio risk WITHOUT sacrificing return by combining assets that don't move together perfectly. Diversification is the only free lunch in finance. The efficient frontier shows the best possible risk-return combinations.

Efficient Frontier
Capital Market Line
Correlation Effect

ConceptWhat it means
Efficient FrontierSet of portfolios with maximum return for each level of risk. Any portfolio below the frontier is suboptimal.
Minimum Variance PortfolioThe leftmost point on the frontier — lowest possible risk for the given assets. Often ~60/40 stocks/bonds in practice.
Capital Market Line (CML)Line from risk-free rate tangent to the frontier. Every point on the CML dominates points on the frontier at equal risk.
Sharpe Ratio(Portfolio Return − Risk-free Rate) / Std Dev. Measures return per unit of risk. The slope of a line from the risk-free rate to your portfolio.
Correlation (ρ)The lower the correlation between assets, the more diversification benefit. ρ = −1 = perfect hedge. ρ = +1 = no benefit.

5. Sequence of Returns Risk

The average return matters less than the ORDER of returns — especially in retirement when you're withdrawing money. Getting bad returns early in retirement is catastrophic even if the long-run average is fine. This is why your 20s and 30s are actually GOOD times to experience bear markets.

7. Anatomy of a Bear Market & Drawdown Recovery

Every investor will experience multiple 20–50% drawdowns in their lifetime. Understanding what they look like and how long recovery takes helps you stay invested when it's hardest.

Historical Drawdowns
Recovery Math
Stocks vs Bonds in Crisis

Recovery asymmetry: A 50% loss requires a 100% gain to break even. A 25% loss requires a 33% gain. This is why avoiding the deepest drawdowns matters, and why position sizing and diversification are not just theory — they directly affect whether you survive long enough for the recovery.

8. Valuation Multiples & Market Levels

Valuations tell you what you're paying per dollar of earnings (or book value, sales, cash flow). They don't predict short-term direction, but they are the single best long-run return predictor: buy cheap, earn more over a decade. Buy expensive, earn less.

9. Correlation & Portfolio Diversification

Correlation measures how two assets move together (−1 to +1). Lower correlation = more diversification benefit = lower portfolio volatility without sacrificing return. Most asset classes become more correlated during crises — when you need diversification most.
