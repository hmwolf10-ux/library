Power User Shortcuts & Hidden Features

The shortcuts most people never discover — across macOS, Safari, iPhone, and iOS. Same idea as the Excel doc: stuff that actually saves time.

macOS — System & Window Management

The shortcuts that make you stop reaching for the mouse constantly.

Spotlight (Cmd+Space) alone is worth learning everything on this page. Most Mac users have never used it beyond opening apps — it does math, unit conversion, dictionary lookups, file search, and web search without touching the browser.

macOS — Text & Cursor Navigation

Mac text navigation is more powerful than most people realize — especially the Ctrl shortcuts that work in every text field system-wide.

Option+Arrow to jump word by word and Option+Delete to delete whole words are the two most time-saving text shortcuts on Mac. Once habitual, you'll never go back to holding backspace.

macOS — Productivity & Hidden Features

The Option key is a goldmine on Mac — hold it in any menu to reveal hidden options most users never see.

Hold Option in any menu bar item or dropdown menu. The hidden options this reveals vary by app but are almost always worth knowing — especially in Finder, the Apple menu, and any app you use daily.

Safari — Power User

Safari is more powerful than most people use it. These shortcuts apply on both Mac and iPad.

Reader Mode (Cmd+Shift+R) is transformative for reading anything online — strips all the noise and gives you clean text. Combine with Reading List (Cmd+Shift+D) to save articles for offline reading later.

Cmd + Option + FJump to search field / find on page

Cmd + 1 through 9Jump directly to tabs 1–9 by position
Cmd + [ or Cmd + ]Navigate Back / Forward through history
Cmd + Shift + REnter Reader Mode — strips ads, sidebars, and clutter for clean reading

iPhone — Hidden Features & Gestures

iOS has a massive number of features buried in gestures, long presses, and settings most people never touch.

The spacebar trackpad trick alone (long press spacebar → cursor repositioning) will save you significant frustration. Back Tap takes 30 seconds to set up and becomes one of your most-used interactions. Both are worth doing today.

Settings Worth Actually Changing

Most people set up their iPhone once and never go back into Settings. These are the specific settings that meaningfully improve daily use, battery life, or privacy.
Shortcut / FeatureWhat It Does
Settings → Screen Time → App LimitsSet daily time limits per app category — actually effective at reducing mindless scrolling when you take it seriously
Settings → Focus ModesBeyond Do Not Disturb — create custom Focus modes (Work, Sleep, Study) that filter notifications, hide app pages, and auto-activate on schedule
Settings → Battery → Battery HealthSee your battery's maximum capacity — below 80% means it's time to replace it. Significant performance impact below this threshold.
Settings → Privacy → Location ServicesAudit every app's location access. Most apps don't need it. Set to 'While Using' or 'Never' for anything that doesn't obviously need it.
Settings → General → Transfer or Reset iPhone → Reset → Reset All SettingsFixes most weird iPhone behavior without wiping your data — resets network settings, display settings, etc. but keeps all apps and photos
Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Reduce MotionReduces animation throughout iOS — faster feel and less battery consumption
Settings → Siri & Search (per app)Disable Siri suggestions and search for apps you don't want surfaced — cleaner Spotlight results
Settings → Notifications → Scheduled SummaryBatch non-urgent notifications into morning and evening summaries instead of constant interruptions throughout the day
Settings → Face ID & Passcode → Allow Access When LockedControl exactly what's accessible without unlocking — Wallet, Control Center, Notification Center, etc.
Settings → General → AirDropSet to 'Contacts Only' — 'Everyone' is a security risk in public and causes unwanted AirDrop requests

iOS Productivity & Apple Ecosystem

The real power of Apple devices is how they work together — most people only use a fraction of it.

Universal Clipboard, Handoff, AirDrop, and Continuity Camera are genuinely seamless when you're in the Apple ecosystem. If you use both an iPhone and Mac and aren't using these, you're leaving significant daily time savings on the table.
Shortcut / FeatureWhat It Does
Swipe up from bottom (or press Home)Go Home
Swipe up from bottom, pause mid-screenApp Switcher — see all open apps, swipe up on any to close
Swipe left / right along the bottom barSwitch between recently used apps without opening the switcher — fastest app switching method
Ask Siri 'Open Settings' or any app nameFaster than hunting through pages of apps for something you rarely use
Siri: 'Remind me about this'While viewing anything (email, webpage, note), say this and Siri creates a reminder that links back to the exact content
Swipe right from left edge (in most apps)Go back — works in Safari, Mail, Settings, most Apple apps. Faster than reaching for the Back button.
Long press Share button in SafariAdd to Home Screen — creates a web app icon. Useful for sites you use constantly (bank, work tools).
Pinch photos in Camera Roll togetherZooms out to show months / years view for faster navigation through your photo library
In Calculator, swipe left on the number displayDeletes the last digit — lets you fix typos without clearing the entire calculation
In Maps, tap the blue dot (your location)Shows your current speed, elevation, floor level (in buildings), and opens location sharing options
iPhone as Webcam for Mac (Continuity Camera)In any video call on Mac, your iPhone can be the camera — dramatically better quality. Just have both devices nearby and signed into the same Apple ID.
Universal ClipboardCopy on iPhone, paste on Mac (or vice versa) — works automatically when both are on the same Wi-Fi and Apple ID. No setup needed.
AirDrop (between iPhone and Mac)The fastest way to move any file, photo, or link between Apple devices. Faster than email or iCloud for large files.
HandoffStart something on iPhone, finish on Mac — works with Safari, Mail, Notes, Maps, Messages. Look for the app icon in the Mac's Dock when your iPhone is nearby.
