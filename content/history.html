The Long Arc — World History Timeline

PeriodDatesWhat HappenedWhy It Matters
Prehistoric / Out of Africa~300,000 – 10,000 BCEHomo sapiens evolve in Africa, spread globally. Hunter-gatherer societies. Language, fire, art.The cognitive revolution (Sapiens can think in abstractions) is the foundational event — gave us myth, cooperation at scale, and eventually civilization.
Agricultural Revolution~10,000 – 5,000 BCEDomestication of plants and animals. Permanent settlements. Population explosion. Inequality begins.The single biggest change in human lifestyle until the Industrial Revolution. Enabled cities, states, and complex societies — but also hierarchy, disease, and harder labor.
Bronze Age Civilizations~3,500 – 1,200 BCEMesopotamia (Sumerians, Babylonians), Egypt, Indus Valley, Shang Dynasty China. Writing, law codes, trade networks.First writing (cuneiform ~3400 BCE), first cities (Uruk), first code of law (Hammurabi). The blueprint for civilization.
Bronze Age Collapse~1,200 – 1,150 BCEWithin 50 years, nearly every major Eastern Mediterranean civilization collapses. Egypt weakens, Hittites gone, Mycenae gone, trade networks collapse.One of history's great mysteries — possibly Sea Peoples, drought, earthquakes, systems failure. A warning about civilizational fragility.
Axial Age~800 – 200 BCESimultaneous intellectual revolution across civilizations: Confucius, Buddha, Socrates, Plato, Aristotle, Isaiah, Zoroaster. Philosophy, ethics, world religions emerge.Karl Jaspers coined the term. The moral and philosophical foundations of every major civilization were laid in this 600-year window.
Classical Antiquity~500 BCE – 500 CEAthens, Sparta, Persian Wars, Alexander the Great (356–323 BCE), Roman Republic and Empire, Han Dynasty China, Maurya Empire India.Democracy, philosophy, Roman law, engineering, Christianity — the foundational layer of Western civilization. Rome at its peak controlled ~20% of the world's population.
Fall of Rome / Dark Ages~400 – 900 CEWestern Roman Empire collapses 476 CE. Islamic Golden Age begins (~750 CE). Europe fragments into feudal kingdoms. Byzantine Empire survives in East.Not actually "dark" globally — Islamic scholars preserve Greek knowledge, China thrives. Europe loses literacy, trade, and urban life for centuries.
Medieval Period~900 – 1400 CECrusades (1095–1291), Mongol conquests (1206–1368), Black Death (1347–1351, killed 30–60% of Europe), Gothic cathedrals, universities, Magna Carta (1215).The Black Death may have triggered the Renaissance — massive labor shortage → higher wages → merchant class → patronage of arts. Catastrophe creates opportunity.
Age of Discovery~1400 – 1600 CEPortuguese and Spanish exploration. Columbus 1492. Magellan circumnavigation. Colonization of Americas. Columbian Exchange (crops, disease, gold).The most consequential event in globalization history. The Columbian Exchange killed ~90% of indigenous Americans (disease) — the largest demographic collapse in human history. It also brought potatoes, tomatoes, and corn to the Old World, enabling European population growth.
Scientific Revolution~1543 – 1700 CECopernicus, Galileo, Kepler, Newton. Empiricism replaces theological authority. Scientific method emerges.Changed how humans understand knowledge itself. Authority no longer sufficient — evidence required. Led directly to the Enlightenment and Industrial Revolution.
Enlightenment~1685 – 1800 CELocke, Voltaire, Rousseau, Hume, Kant. Reason, individual rights, secularism, democracy. American and French Revolutions.The philosophical foundation of modern liberal democracy. The US Declaration of Independence is a direct product of Enlightenment thinking.
Industrial Revolution~1760 – 1900 CESteam engine, factory system, railroads, urbanization. Britain first, then Europe and America. GDP per capita begins exponential growth.Ended the Malthusian trap — for 10,000 years, population growth ate all productivity gains. Industrialization finally broke this. The material wealth you live in is its legacy.
Age of Imperialism~1800 – 1914 CEEuropean powers colonize Africa, Asia. Britain controls 25% of Earth's land. Opium Wars, Scramble for Africa.Created the global power structures, borders, and inequalities that still define geopolitics today. Most modern conflicts have colonial roots.
World War I1914 – 1918Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand triggers alliance system. Trench warfare, 20M+ deaths. Ottoman, Austro-Hungarian, Russian, German empires all collapse.The "Seminal Catastrophe" of the 20th century. Led directly to WWII. Set borders for the Middle East that still cause conflict today (Sykes-Picot, Balfour).
Interwar Period / Great Depression1918 – 1939Hyperinflation in Germany (1923), Great Depression (1929), rise of fascism, Stalin's collectivization and purges.The Depression wasn't just economic — it was the petri dish for totalitarianism. Desperate people vote for radical solutions. The lesson every central banker carries.
World War II1939 – 1945Hitler invades Europe; Japan conquers Asia-Pacific. Holocaust (6M+ Jews). 70–85M total deaths. Atomic bombs on Japan. US emerges as dominant superpower.The defining event of the 20th century. Every international institution (UN, IMF, World Bank, NATO) was built in its aftermath specifically to prevent repetition.
Cold War1947 – 1991US vs. USSR ideological competition. Korean War, Cuban Missile Crisis (1962 — humanity's closest call with nuclear war), Vietnam, proxy wars worldwide, Space Race.Shaped everything: US military spending, global alliances, the emergence of the developing world's post-colonial states, technological innovation (the internet began as ARPANET).
Post-Cold War / Globalization1991 – 2001Soviet collapse, "End of History" (Fukuyama), China's rise, dot-com boom, NAFTA, WTO.The "unipolar moment" — US power unchallenged. Free trade ideology dominant. Planted seeds of the populism, inequality, and China rivalry that define today.
The 21st Century2001 – Present9/11, Global War on Terror, 2008 Financial Crisis, China overtaking Japan as #2 economy (2010), COVID-19 pandemic (2020), AI revolution (2022–), Russia-Ukraine war (2022), US-China rivalry intensifying.The defining question: Is this a new era of great power competition, or can a rules-based international order survive?

The Great Empires

EmpirePeak EraGreatest ExtentKey Innovation / Legacy
Persian (Achaemenid)550–330 BCEFrom Egypt to India — first truly multiethnic empireCyrus Cylinder (first human rights declaration). Tolerance policy. Road system (Royal Road). Postal service.
Greek / Macedonian (Alexander)336–323 BCEGreece to Egypt to India — in 13 yearsHellenism — spread Greek culture, language, philosophy across three continents. Alexandria becomes the intellectual capital of the world.
Roman27 BCE – 476 CE (West)Britain to Mesopotamia — ~5M km²Roman law (basis of most European legal systems). Latin (ancestor of French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Romanian). Christianity as state religion. Engineering (aqueducts, roads, concrete).
Tang Dynasty China618 – 907 CELargest empire in the world at the timeGolden age of Chinese arts, poetry, and technology. The Silk Road reached its peak. Buddhism flourishes. Population ~50M — world's largest country.
Islamic Caliphate (Abbasid)750 – 1258 CESpain to Central AsiaIslamic Golden Age: preserved and advanced Greek knowledge, invented algebra (Al-Khwarizmi), advanced medicine, astronomy, optics. The House of Wisdom in Baghdad.
Mongol1206 – 1368 CELargest contiguous land empire in history — ~24M km²The Pax Mongolica — relative peace across Eurasia enabled unprecedented trade and cultural exchange (Marco Polo). Also: mass destruction of cities, end of the Abbasid Caliphate (1258), possible redirection of history in Europe.
Ottoman1299 – 1922 CESoutheast Europe, Middle East, North AfricaLongest-lasting empire in history. Fall of Constantinople (1453) ended the Byzantine Empire and is often taken as the start of the modern era. Its collapse created the modern Middle East.
Spanish1492 – 1898 CEAmericas, Philippines, parts of Europe — first truly global empireColonization of the Americas, Spanish language (400M+ speakers), Catholicism across the Western Hemisphere. Silver from Peru funded 16th century Europe.
British1815 – 1945 CE~35M km² at peak — "The sun never sets"Common law, English language (global lingua franca), industrialization exported globally, the foundations of India, Australia, Canada, the modern Middle East. Created the Pax Britannica.
American (Informal)1945 – PresentNo territorial empire but military presence in 750+ bases, dollar is global reserve currency, Hollywood, internetThe rules-based international order (UN, IMF, World Bank, WTO), nuclear deterrence, the global trading system. The most powerful hegemon in human history.

Turning Points — Events That Changed Everything

EventDateWhy It's a Turning Point
Invention of Writing (Cuneiform)~3400 BCEEnabled record-keeping, law, literature, history — civilization itself. Before writing, no complex society can function at scale.
Battle of Marathon490 BCEAthens defeats the Persian invasion. Had Persia won, Western philosophy, democracy, and classical Greek culture may never have flourished.
Death of Alexander the Great323 BCEDies at 32 with no clear succession. His generals split the empire. Changed the political map of the ancient world forever.
Fall of Western Rome476 CETraditional marker of the end of antiquity. Europe enters a period of fragmentation, reduced literacy, and lost urban life.
Black Death1347–1351Kills 30–60% of Europe's population. Shatters feudalism (labor shortage), drives the Renaissance, triggers a crisis of faith that enables the Reformation.
Gutenberg's Printing Press1440Mass production of books. Enables the Reformation (Luther's theses spread instantly), the Scientific Revolution (scholars share work), and eventually democracy (informed citizenry).
Columbus Lands in the Americas1492Connects two hemispheres isolated for 10,000 years. Columbian Exchange transforms global agriculture, population, economics, and power.
Assassination of Franz FerdinandJune 28, 1914Triggers WWI via the alliance system. Reshapes the entire 20th century.
HiroshimaAugust 6, 1945First use of nuclear weapons. Created the nuclear deterrence paradigm that has (so far) prevented WWIII between great powers.
Fall of the Berlin WallNovember 9, 1989Symbolizes the end of the Cold War, the collapse of Soviet communism, and the "unipolar moment" of American dominance.
World Wide Web goes public1991Tim Berners-Lee releases HTTP and HTML. The internet becomes accessible to everyone. Everything that follows — e-commerce, social media, AI, remote work — traces back to this.

Patterns in History

History doesn't repeat, but it rhymes. These structural patterns appear again and again across different civilizations and eras:

PatternHow It WorksHistorical Examples
The Rise and Fall of EmpiresEmpires rise through military/economic strength, overextend, face internal division, and collapse under financial strain or external pressure. Average great power cycle: 200–300 years.Rome, Ottoman, British, Soviet. Dalio's "Big Cycle" maps this to debt and internal order.
Debt-Driven Boom and BustCredit expands, fuels prosperity, exceeds the ability to repay, then contracts painfully. Central banks were created to manage this but can only delay, not prevent.1929 Great Depression, Tulip Mania (1637), South Sea Bubble (1720), 2008 Financial Crisis, Japan 1989
Plague as AccelerantMajor disease outbreaks don't just kill — they disrupt power structures, accelerate existing trends, and create opportunities for new ideas.Black Death → Renaissance + Reformation. COVID-19 → remote work, AI acceleration, supply chain rethinking.
Technology as DisruptionMajor technological shifts destroy existing power structures and create new ones. Early movers capture disproportionate gains; those who resist are marginalized.Printing press vs. Catholic Church. Industrialization vs. artisans. Internet vs. newspapers. AI vs. knowledge workers.
Great Power CompetitionWhen a rising power challenges a dominant hegemon, war becomes likely (Thucydides Trap). This has played out ~70% of the time historically.Athens vs. Sparta, England vs. France (multiple), Germany vs. Britain (WWI/WWII), US vs. USSR, now US vs. China
Internal Wealth Inequality → UpheavalAs inequality reaches extremes, populist movements emerge — either reform or revolution. The middle class is the stabilizing force; when it collapses, so does political stability.French Revolution, Russian Revolution, 1930s fascism, Arab Spring
