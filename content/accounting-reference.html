The language of business. Every financial statement, every ratio, every valuation starts here.

The Accounting Equation

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

This is immovable. Every transaction in accounting is just a rearrangement of this equation. If you buy equipment with cash, assets go up (equipment) and assets go down (cash) — net zero. If you take a loan, assets go up (cash) and liabilities go up (loan) — equation still balances.

CategoryWhat It IsExamples
AssetsEverything the company owns or is owedCash, receivables, inventory, PP&E, intangibles, goodwill
LiabilitiesEverything the company owes to othersAccounts payable, accrued expenses, debt, deferred revenue
EquityWhat's left for shareholders (Assets − Liabilities)Common stock, retained earnings, additional paid-in capital, AOCI

Debits & Credits — The Core Mechanic

Every transaction hits at least two accounts (double-entry). Debits and credits are just directions — they don't mean good or bad.

The rule: Debits increase assets and expenses. Credits increase liabilities, equity, and revenue. The opposite decreases each.
Account TypeDebitCreditNormal Balance
AssetsIncrease ↑Decrease ↓Debit
LiabilitiesDecrease ↓Increase ↑Credit
EquityDecrease ↓Increase ↑Credit
RevenueDecrease ↓Increase ↑Credit
ExpensesIncrease ↑Decrease ↓Debit

Memory trick (DEALER): Dividends, Expenses, Assets = Debit to increase. Liabilities, Equity, Revenue = Credit to increase.

The Three Financial Statements

The income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement are three lenses on the same business. They're interconnected — changes in one flow through to the others.

Income Statement (P&L)

Shows profitability over a period of time (quarter, year).

Line ItemWhat It Is
Revenue (Net Sales)Total sales, net of returns and discounts. The top line.
Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)Direct cost of producing what was sold — materials, labor, manufacturing.
Gross ProfitRevenue − COGS. Shows how efficiently the product is made/delivered.
Operating Expenses (OpEx)SG&A, R&D, depreciation, amortization — running the business.
Operating Income (EBIT)Gross Profit − OpEx. The core business profit before interest and taxes.
Interest Expense / IncomeCost of debt, or income from cash holdings.
EBT (Pre-tax Income)EBIT ± interest. What gets taxed.
Income Tax ExpenseTaxes owed on EBT. Effective rate ≠ statutory rate (deferred taxes, credits).
Net IncomeThe bottom line. Flows into retained earnings on the balance sheet.
Key metric shortcuts: EBITDA = EBIT + D&A. Used in valuation because it approximates cash earnings before capital structure. Gross margin = Gross Profit / Revenue. Operating margin = EBIT / Revenue.

Balance Sheet

Snapshot of what the company owns and owes at a single point in time. Always: Assets = Liabilities + Equity.

SectionLine ItemsNotes
Current AssetsCash, Marketable Securities, Accounts Receivable, Inventory, Prepaid ExpensesConvertible to cash within 12 months
Non-Current AssetsPP&E (net), Intangibles, Goodwill, Long-term Investments, Deferred Tax AssetsLong-lived assets; PP&E is net of accumulated depreciation
Current LiabilitiesAccounts Payable, Accrued Expenses, Short-term Debt, Deferred Revenue, Current portion of LTDDue within 12 months
Non-Current LiabilitiesLong-term Debt, Deferred Tax Liabilities, Pension Obligations, Lease LiabilitiesDue beyond 12 months
EquityCommon Stock, APIC, Retained Earnings, Treasury Stock, AOCIRetained earnings = accumulated net income − dividends paid

Cash Flow Statement

Shows actual cash movement — where it came from and where it went. The statement sophisticated investors read first.

SectionWhat's IncludedKey Signals
Operating Activities (CFO)Net income adjusted for non-cash items (D&A), changes in working capital (A/R, inventory, A/P)Positive CFO = business generates real cash. CFO > Net Income = quality earnings. CFO < Net Income consistently = earnings quality issue.
Investing Activities (CFI)CapEx (buying PP&E), acquisitions, proceeds from asset sales, investments in securitiesNegative CFI is normal for growing companies. CapEx as % of revenue shows capital intensity.
Financing Activities (CFF)Debt issuance/repayment, equity issuance, share buybacks, dividends paidShows how the company funds itself and returns capital to shareholders.
Free Cash Flow (FCF)CFO − CapExThe real cash available to service debt, pay dividends, buy back stock, or invest. Most important metric for valuation.

How the Three Statements Link

ConnectionHow It Works
Net Income → Balance SheetNet income increases retained earnings in equity (or decreases if a loss)
Net Income → Cash FlowCash flow statement starts with net income, then adjusts for non-cash items and working capital changes
CapEx → Balance SheetCapEx increases PP&E on the balance sheet; then depreciation slowly reduces it back each period
Depreciation → Income + CFD&A is an expense on the income statement (reduces net income) but added back on the CF statement (non-cash)
Debt → Balance Sheet + CFNew debt increases cash (asset) and liability on balance sheet; interest expense flows through income statement
Working Capital ChangesIncreases in A/R (customers haven't paid yet) reduce cash flow; increases in A/P (haven't paid vendors) increase cash flow

Key Accounting Concepts & GAAP Principles

ConceptWhat It MeansWhy It Matters
Accrual AccountingRevenue recognized when earned, expenses when incurred — not when cash changes handsGives a more accurate picture of economic activity; required under GAAP. Cash accounting is simpler but misleading for big businesses.
Revenue Recognition (ASC 606)Revenue is recognized when (or as) performance obligations are satisfied — i.e., when the customer gets what they paid forCritical for SaaS (subscription vs. upfront), construction (% completion), software licenses. Timing of revenue recognition moves the whole P&L.
Matching PrincipleExpenses should be recognized in the same period as the revenue they helped generateWhy you capitalize and depreciate assets instead of expensing them immediately; why COGS is only recognized when the product sells
ConservatismWhen uncertain, record losses/expenses sooner and revenues/gains laterPrevents overstating financial health. "Anticipate losses, not gains."
Going ConcernAssumes the company will continue operating indefinitelyAuditors flag going concern issues if there's substantial doubt — a major red flag for investors
MaterialityOnly information that would affect a reasonable investor's decision needs to be disclosedSmall errors don't require restatement; large ones do. Judgment call with legal implications.
ConsistencyUse the same accounting methods period to periodChanges in accounting method (e.g., FIFO to LIFO) require disclosure and can distort comparisons

Inventory Methods

MethodHow It WorksIncome Statement EffectUsed When
FIFO (First In, First Out)Oldest inventory is assumed sold firstIn rising cost environment: lower COGS, higher gross profit, higher taxesMost US companies; perishables; when you want higher reported earnings
LIFO (Last In, First Out)Newest inventory is assumed sold firstIn rising cost environment: higher COGS, lower gross profit, lower taxes (tax shield)Only allowed under US GAAP (not IFRS); useful in inflationary periods for tax savings
Weighted AverageAverage cost of all units in inventorySmooths out cost fluctuationsCommodities and interchangeable goods

Depreciation Methods

MethodFormulaWhen to UseEffect
Straight-Line(Cost − Salvage Value) / Useful LifeMost common; predictable assetsEqual expense each year; simplest
Double-Declining Balance (DDB)2 × (1/Useful Life) × Book ValueAssets that lose value fast early (vehicles, tech)Front-loads expenses; reduces taxes early
Units of Production(Cost − Salvage) / Total Units × Units UsedManufacturing equipment tied to outputExpense tracks actual usage
MACRSIRS-mandated accelerated schedulesUS tax returns onlyAccelerated; different from GAAP book depreciation
Book vs. Tax: Companies keep two sets of books — GAAP books (for investors) and tax books (for the IRS). Timing differences create Deferred Tax Assets (paid too much tax) or Deferred Tax Liabilities (paid too little tax so far). These live on the balance sheet.

Working Capital

Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities

The short-term liquidity cushion. Positive = can cover near-term obligations. Negative = potential cash crunch.

MetricFormulaWhat It Tells You
Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)(A/R / Revenue) × 365How long it takes to collect cash from customers. Rising DSO = collection problem.
Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO)(Inventory / COGS) × 365How long inventory sits before being sold. Rising DIO = demand softness or overstocking.
Days Payable Outstanding (DPO)(A/P / COGS) × 365How long the company takes to pay its suppliers. Higher DPO = better cash retention.
Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)DSO + DIO − DPOHow many days cash is tied up in operations. Lower (or negative) = more efficient. Amazon runs negative CCC.
