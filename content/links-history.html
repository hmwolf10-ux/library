Free Books to Download

BookAuthorDownloadWhy Read It
The History of the Decline and Fall of the Roman EmpireEdward GibbonProject GutenbergThe greatest historical work in the English language. 6 volumes. Gibbon traces Rome's fall over 1,500 years — still relevant for any thinking about civilizational decline.
The Outline of HistoryH.G. WellsProject GutenbergWells wrote this immediately after WWI as a call for global perspective. A full narrative of human history from life's origins to the 1920s. Opinionated and readable.
The Story of MankindHendrik Van LoonProject GutenbergFirst book to win the Newbery Medal (1922). Enormously readable world history. Great starting point.
Democracy in AmericaAlexis de TocquevilleProject GutenbergFrench aristocrat visits America in 1831 and writes the most accurate analysis of American democracy ever written. Eerily prescient about social media, populism, and individualism.
The PrinceMachiavelliProject Gutenberg1513. The original political science text. Ruthlessly practical — how power is actually acquired and held. Read this to understand political behavior.
MeditationsMarcus AureliusProject GutenbergPrivate journal of a Roman emperor who was also a Stoic philosopher. The most practical philosophy book ever written — about how to live, not just what to believe.
The Art of WarSun TzuProject Gutenberg~500 BCE. 13 chapters on strategy, deception, and knowing your enemy. Still taught at West Point and every major business school. Read in an hour.
Common SenseThomas PaineProject Gutenberg1776. The pamphlet that convinced Americans to declare independence. The most influential piece of political writing in American history.
