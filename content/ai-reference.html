How Neural Networks Work

A neural network is loosely inspired by the brain — layers of interconnected "neurons" that transform an input into an output by learning which connections to strengthen.

Basic Architecture

ComponentWhat It Does
Input LayerTakes in raw data — pixels, tokens, numbers. Each neuron = one feature.
Hidden LayersWhere the learning happens. Each layer learns increasingly abstract representations. Early layers detect edges; later layers detect faces.
Output LayerProduces the prediction — a probability distribution over classes, a number, a token.
Weights & BiasesThe parameters the network learns. A network with 1B parameters has 1 billion adjustable knobs.
Activation FunctionNon-linearity applied after each neuron (ReLU, sigmoid, GELU). Without this, many layers = one layer.
BackpropagationThe training algorithm. Computes how much each weight contributed to the error, then adjusts it in the right direction (gradient descent).
Loss FunctionMeasures how wrong the prediction is. Training minimizes this over millions of examples.

Key Neural Network Types

ArchitectureBest ForHow It WorksExamples
CNN (Convolutional Neural Network)Images, spatial dataLearns local patterns through filters that scan across the image. Translation-invariant.Image recognition (ResNet, VGG), medical imaging, self-driving vision
RNN / LSTMSequential data (pre-2017)Processes sequences step by step, maintaining a hidden state as "memory"Early NLP, time series forecasting, speech recognition
TransformerText, code, images, audio — almost everything nowAttention mechanism — every token attends to every other token simultaneously. Parallelizable. Scales massively.GPT, Claude, BERT, Whisper, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion
GAN (Generative Adversarial Network)Image generation (older)Generator vs. Discriminator compete — generator tries to fool, discriminator tries to detect. Nash equilibrium = realistic images.DeepFakes, StyleGAN, image synthesis

Large Language Models (LLMs)

The most important AI development since deep learning. LLMs are transformer-based models trained on massive text datasets to predict the next token. That's it — but it turns out that doing this at scale produces models that can reason, code, write, and learn in context.

How LLMs Work — The Key Ideas

ConceptWhat It Means
TokenizationText is split into tokens (roughly words/subwords). GPT-4 uses ~100K tokens. "ChatGPT" might be 2 tokens: "Chat" + "GPT".
Context WindowHow much text the model can "see" at once when generating. GPT-4: 128K tokens. Claude 3.5: 200K tokens. Longer = can handle longer docs.
Attention MechanismThe core of the Transformer. For each token, the model learns which other tokens to pay attention to. Lets it understand relationships across long distances in text.
Pre-trainingTrain on hundreds of billions of tokens from the internet, books, code. Predicting next word forces the model to learn grammar, facts, reasoning, code — everything in the training data.
Fine-tuningTake a pre-trained model and train it further on a specific task or style with curated data. Makes it better at that task without forgetting everything else.
RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback)Human raters rank outputs → train a reward model → use RL to push the LLM toward outputs humans prefer. How ChatGPT was made safe and helpful. Without this, models can be unhinged.
ParametersThe "size" of the model. GPT-3: 175B params. GPT-4: rumored ~1T. More params ≠ always better — architecture, data, and training matter equally.
Emergent BehaviorCapabilities that appear at scale with no explicit training — multi-step reasoning, few-shot learning, chain-of-thought. Not well understood; one of the active research frontiers.
HallucinationLLMs generate plausible-sounding but false information confidently. A fundamental limitation — they're pattern-matching machines, not truth databases.
TemperatureControls randomness in generation. Temperature=0: always picks highest-probability token (deterministic). Temperature=1: samples from the full distribution (creative but less reliable).

Prompting — Getting More Out of AI

TechniqueWhat It DoesExample
Zero-shotAsk directly with no examples. Works surprisingly well for capable models."Summarize this earnings call in 3 bullet points."
Few-shotProvide examples of the desired input/output format before your real request.Show 2-3 examples of how you want the output formatted, then give the real input.
Chain-of-Thought (CoT)Ask the model to "think step by step" before answering. Dramatically improves reasoning accuracy."Let's think through this step by step..." or just end with "Think carefully before answering."
Role / PersonaTell the model who to be. Changes the framing and expertise level."You are an expert portfolio manager analyzing this 10-K..."
System PromptInstructions baked in before the conversation. Sets behavior, tone, constraints.Used by developers building products; the "hidden instructions" behind every AI product
RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation)Feed the model relevant documents as context to ground its answers in real data.Claude searching your PDFs before answering a question about them

AI Training — The Big Picture

Scaling Laws

OpenAI researchers found in 2020 (Kaplan et al.) that model performance scales predictably with three things: compute, data, and model size — following power laws. This was the green light to keep scaling. Chinchilla (DeepMind, 2022) refined this: you should scale data and model size proportionally, not just throw more parameters at it.

Key AI Safety Concepts

ConceptWhat It Is
AlignmentMaking AI systems do what humans intend. A model can be capable but misaligned (pursues the wrong objective).
InterpretabilityUnderstanding why a model does what it does. Current models are largely black boxes. Anthropic, DeepMind, and academia are working on this.
Constitutional AIAnthropic's technique: give the model a set of principles and have it self-critique against them. Less reliance on human raters.
AI Risk (Long-term)Debate between those concerned about superintelligent AI pursuing goals misaligned with humans ("p(doom)" crowd) and those focused on near-term harms (bias, misuse, surveillance).
AI GovernanceUS EO on AI, EU AI Act, voluntary commitments by frontier labs. Rapidly evolving. Who regulates AI and how is one of the biggest policy questions of the decade.
