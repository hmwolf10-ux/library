The Points Game — Travel for Free

Credit card points are the single biggest travel hack available. Done right, you can fly business class internationally for the cost of economy, or stay at $500/night hotels for $0. The key is signing up for the right cards, hitting the signup bonus, and knowing how to redeem.

Tricks That Actually Work

  • Use incognito mode. Airlines may track repeat searches and raise prices. Unproven but zero cost to do it.
  • Book one-way when it's cheaper. Sometimes two one-ways beat a round trip. Always check both.
  • Hidden city ticketing. Book a flight with a layover where you actually want to go, then don't board the connection. Legal but airlines hate it; don't do it with checked bags.
  • Check nearby airports. Flying into Midway (MDW) vs. O'Hare (ORD). Flying out of Newark vs. JFK. Can save hundreds.
  • Book internationally for international trips. Sometimes booking on the airline's website for the destination country is cheaper.

How to Book Hotels Cheaply

  • Book direct after finding price on Kayak. Hotels often price-match or offer perks (breakfast, upgrade) when booking direct.
  • Use HotelsCombined or Trivago to scan all OTAs simultaneously.
  • Hostel + private room for solo travel on a budget. Hostelworld has reviews. Private rooms in well-rated hostels are often cleaner and more social than budget hotels.
  • Airbnb for groups (3+ people) or longer stays (7+ days = significant discount). Better kitchen access, more space.
  • Call the hotel directly for multi-night stays. Front desk managers often have authority to discount that OTAs don't.

Packing — The Carry-On Only System

Checking bags is the enemy of smooth travel. It costs money, wastes 30+ minutes, and creates anxiety. Everything below fits in a 40L carry-on for 2 weeks.

The System

  • One 40L backpack (Osprey Farpoint 40, Peak Design Travel Backpack, or Away Carry-On). Must fit in overhead bin.
  • Packing cubes to compress and organize. Eagle Creek is the standard.
  • Tops: 3–4 merino wool t-shirts (Uniqlo or Woolly). Air-dry overnight. Don't smell. Pack 3 and rotate.
  • Bottoms: 2 pairs of pants (one smart, one casual). Shorts if warm weather.
  • Shoes: Wear your biggest shoes on the plane. One pair of dress/casual shoes in the bag max.
  • Toiletries: TSA-approved bag with 3oz liquids. Buy shampoo/soap at destination for trips over a week.
  • Electronics: Laptop, phone, AirPods, universal adapter, portable battery. Keep cords together in a small pouch.
  • Documents: Digital copies of passport, insurance, and all bookings in Google Drive or email. Print key ones.

Merino Wool — Why It's the Answer

Merino wool t-shirts are temperature-regulating, moisture-wicking, odor-resistant, and look decent in restaurants and bars. You can wear them 3+ days. Brands: Uniqlo (budget), Wool&Prince (mid), Outlier (premium).

Logistics — The Things That Go Wrong

Money & Payments

SituationBest Move
Currency exchangeUse ATMs at your destination (not airport ATMs, not exchange booths). Schwab checking account refunds all ATM fees worldwide. Charles Schwab High Yield Investor Checking is the best travel debit card.
Credit card abroadUse a card with no foreign transaction fees (Chase Sapphire, Amex, Capital One Venture). Never use debit internationally unless necessary.
Emergency cashAlways keep $100–200 USD equivalent in local cash for places that don't take cards (rural areas, small restaurants, taxis).

Safety Basics

  • Use Apple AirTags in each piece of luggage. Saved thousands of travelers from lost bag situations.
  • Money belt for high-theft areas (Barcelona, Rome, tourist markets). Keep minimal cash in wallet; rest in money belt under clothes.
  • Don't flash expensive gear. Camera, laptop, AirPods — keep out of sight in crowded areas.
  • Always share your itinerary with one person at home who knows where you are.
  • Travel insurance for international trips. World Nomads or your credit card's built-in coverage. Cover medical evacuation — a helicopter out of a remote area costs $100K+.
  • Lock your stuff in hostels. Most robberies are opportunistic — a padlock on your locker prevents 99% of them.

Health & Vaccines

  • Check CDC travel health page before every international trip: wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel
  • Hep A + Typhoid for developing world travel. Yellow fever for sub-Saharan Africa + parts of South America. Malaria prophylaxis for high-risk areas.
  • Travel pharmacy checklist: Imodium, Pepto, Cipro (if doctor will prescribe), antihistamine, ibuprofen, blister bandages.
